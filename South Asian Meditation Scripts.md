# South Asian Meditation Scripts

Three record-ready meditation scripts based on topics in the source list:

1. **Joint family** — finding belonging and personal space within a multigenerational home.
2. **Setting boundaries** — expressing needs with clarity, kindness, and less guilt.
3. **Study and work stress** — easing pressure and returning attention to the next manageable step.

These scripts are culturally aware but non-prescriptive. They avoid assuming a particular religion, language, gender, or family arrangement. Suggested delivery is slow and warm, at roughly 90–105 words per minute. The pause cues are part of the recording plan and can be adjusted naturally.

---

## 1. Joint family

**Suggested length:** 5–6 minutes  
**Tone:** warm, reassuring, spacious

### Script

Welcome.

Find a position that feels supported. You may sit upright, rest against a wall, or lie down. Let your hands settle wherever they are comfortable.

If it feels safe, allow your eyes to close. Otherwise, soften your gaze toward one steady point.

Take a slow breath in through the nose… and let the breath leave gently.

Again, breathe in… and breathe out.

[Pause 5 seconds]

You may be surrounded by people you love. You may also be carrying noise, expectations, questions, responsibilities, and very little time that feels completely your own.

For these few minutes, you do not have to solve the family. You do not have to explain yourself. You do not have to choose between belonging and needing space.

For now, simply arrive here.

Notice the places where your body is supported. The chair beneath you. The floor beneath your feet. The bed beneath your back.

Let that support remind you: you are allowed to be held by your surroundings, even when your mind feels pulled in many directions.

[Pause 5 seconds]

Bring awareness to your breath. Do not force it to become deep. Just notice the gentle movement of breathing in… and breathing out.

As you inhale, silently say: **I am here.**

As you exhale, silently say: **I can soften.**

I am here…

I can soften.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Now imagine a small, peaceful space within you. It might be a quiet room, a shaded courtyard, a place beside water, or simply a warm circle of light.

This space is yours. No one is being rejected from it. No one needs to be blamed. It is simply a place where your thoughts can settle and your breathing can belong to you.

Notice that love and space can exist together.

You can care for your family and still have limits.

You can respect elders and still listen to your own body.

You can be grateful for connection and still need quiet.

You can participate in family life without disappearing inside everyone else’s needs.

[Pause 8 seconds]

If guilt appears, meet it gently. Guilt does not always mean you have done something wrong. Sometimes it appears simply because you are learning a new way to care for yourself.

Let the guilt sit beside you without giving it the whole room.

Return to the breath.

Inhale: **My needs are real.**

Exhale: **Other people’s needs are real too.**

There can be room for both.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Now bring to mind one small form of personal space you could create today. Perhaps five quiet minutes in the morning. A walk to the shop. A closed door while you study. Headphones. A cup of tea enjoyed without multitasking. A respectful request for uninterrupted rest.

You do not need to change everything today. One small space is enough to begin.

Silently repeat: **I can belong without abandoning myself.**

Again: **I can belong without abandoning myself.**

[Pause 8 seconds]

Feel the surface beneath you. Notice the temperature of the air. Listen for the sounds around you without needing to judge them.

Let the sounds be part of the world, while your breath remains your quiet centre.

Take one final, comfortable breath in…

And release it slowly.

When you are ready, open your eyes or lift your gaze.

Carry this reminder with you: connection is strongest when it has room for every person, including you.

## 2. Setting boundaries

**Suggested length:** 5–6 minutes  
**Tone:** calm, grounding, encouraging

### Script

Welcome.

Take a moment to settle. Let your shoulders drop by even a small amount. Unclench your jaw. Let your hands rest.

Breathe in slowly…

And breathe out fully.

[Pause 5 seconds]

Many of us learn to be helpful before we learn to notice our own limits. We may be taught to keep the peace, honour the family, avoid disappointing others, or say yes before we have checked whether we have the time and energy.

There is kindness in caring for others.

There is also kindness in telling the truth about what you can and cannot do.

For this practice, you do not need to rehearse a difficult conversation. You only need to notice the quiet signal that tells you: **this is too much for me right now.**

[Pause 8 seconds]

Bring attention to the breath at the centre of your chest. Imagine each inhale creating a little space around your feelings.

Inhale: **I can listen to myself.**

Exhale: **I do not need to answer immediately.**

I can listen to myself…

I do not need to answer immediately.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Picture a gentle boundary around your body. Not a wall. Not a punishment. A clear, kind line that marks where your responsibility ends and another person’s responsibility begins.

Inside this boundary are your time, your energy, your privacy, your body, your money, your attention, and your choices.

You are allowed to care about someone without handing over every one of these things.

You are allowed to pause before agreeing.

You are allowed to change your mind when new information appears.

You are allowed to say no without creating a long defence.

[Pause 8 seconds]

Let one simple sentence come to mind:

**I cannot do that today.**

Or:

**I need time to think before I answer.**

Or:

**I can help for twenty minutes, but I cannot take this on completely.**

Notice what happens in your body as you imagine saying the sentence. Perhaps there is tightness, fear, or guilt.

Breathe around that feeling. You do not have to make the discomfort disappear before you speak honestly.

Confidence often comes after the boundary, not before it.

[Pause 10 seconds]

If you have been taught that a good person is always available, offer yourself a wider definition of goodness.

A good person can be honest.

A good person can rest.

A good person can protect their health.

A good person can disappoint someone and still remain caring.

A good person can say, **not today**, and return another day with more capacity.

[Pause 8 seconds]

Now choose one small boundary for the next twenty-four hours. It might be delaying a reply, protecting your study time, keeping one private matter private, leaving a conversation when it becomes disrespectful, or asking someone else to share a responsibility.

Choose something clear and realistic.

Silently repeat: **My boundary is information, not an attack.**

Again: **My boundary is information, not an attack.**

[Pause 8 seconds]

Return to the feeling of your feet on the floor or your body on the surface beneath you.

Take a slow breath in…

And let it go.

You may be loving and limited. Generous and discerning. Connected and still your own person.

When you are ready, open your eyes.

Take this with you: a clear boundary protects the relationship you have with yourself, and it gives other people the chance to understand what is true.

## 3. Study and work stress

**Suggested length:** 5–6 minutes  
**Tone:** steady, focused, compassionate

### Script

Welcome.

Settle into your seat. If you are at a desk, let both feet touch the floor. If you are resting, allow the surface beneath you to carry your weight.

Let your hands relax.

Breathe in for a comfortable count…

And breathe out a little more slowly.

[Pause 5 seconds]

Stress can make every task feel urgent at the same time. An exam, a deadline, a message from work, family responsibilities, financial concerns, and the fear of falling behind can all arrive in the mind together.

For the next few minutes, none of these problems needs to be solved all at once.

Your only task is to come back to this breath.

Inhale: **This moment is here.**

Exhale: **I can meet one thing at a time.**

This moment is here…

I can meet one thing at a time.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Notice the difference between a task and the story your mind tells about the task.

The task may be: read two pages, answer one email, revise one paragraph, attend one class, complete one form, or make one phone call.

The story may say: **If I cannot do everything, I am failing.**

You do not have to argue with the story. Just notice that it is a thought, not a command.

Let the thought move past like a loud vehicle on a busy road.

Return to the next small action.

[Pause 8 seconds]

Bring awareness to your forehead. Let it soften.

Soften around the eyes.

Unclench the jaw.

Lower the shoulders.

Relax the hands.

Let your belly move naturally as you breathe.

Each release tells the nervous system: **I am allowed to work without attacking myself.**

[Pause 8 seconds]

If comparison appears, remember that South Asian homes and workplaces can carry many visible measures of success: grades, titles, income, promotions, admissions, marriage expectations, and the opinions of relatives or colleagues.

These measures may matter to you, but they are not the whole measure of your life.

Your worth is not waiting at the end of the next result.

For now, let progress mean returning with care.

Returning to the page.

Returning to the problem.

Returning after distraction.

Returning after a difficult day.

[Pause 10 seconds]

Ask yourself gently: **What is the next useful step?**

Do not ask for the perfect step. Do not ask for the step that finishes everything.

Ask for the next useful step.

Allow an answer to appear. It may be very small.

Write a heading. Open the document. Set a ten-minute timer. Drink water. Ask for clarification. Put the phone in another room. Rest, so you can return with a clearer mind.

Choose one step and let it be enough for this moment.

[Pause 8 seconds]

Repeat silently:

**I am more than my productivity.**

**I can move forward gently.**

**One honest step is still progress.**

[Pause 10 seconds]

Take one final breath in…

And release it slowly.

Feel your feet, your hands, and the surface beneath you.

When you are ready, open your eyes.

Carry one question into the rest of your day: **What is the next useful step?**

You do not need to carry the whole future in this breath.
