Scope: three new contemplative replacements for the Noor suit. Source files and ordered outputs remain untouched.

## Shared production direction

Use case: historical-scene / contemplative card illustration.

Asset type: one finished vertical tarot-card artwork, portrait 9:16, artwork only.

Style/medium: painterly cinematic realism fused with the tactile surface of a hand-painted illuminated manuscript; historically coherent Muslim-region material culture; dignified unidentified ordinary people only; modest believable garments; anatomically natural faces and hands.

Composition and deck continuity: one dominant focal subject and one clear symbolic action; quiet negative space; weathered stone, carved cedar, aged brass or silver, marble, water, woven textiles, handmade surfaces; controlled natural light; deep indigo and midnight-blue shadows, ivory, muted teal, terracotta, moonlit silver, restrained brushed gold; subtle purely geometric and botanical ornament; restrained arched framing and a consistent deck border; leave a clean empty lower title cartouche with no text.

Non-negotiable: communicate through atmosphere, posture, water, light, threshold, reflection, and consequence—not arithmetic. The Noor suit is a quiet emotional/light language, never a counted display.

Shared negative constraints: no literal pip layout; no row, fan, grid, wall display, or circle of repeated suit objects; no exact counted collection of cups, lanterns, vessels, or other suit props; no number written or implied by inventory; no lettering, readable text, Arabic script, calligraphy, pseudo-calligraphy, pseudo-glyphs, logos, watermark, UI, border text, or title; no sacred person, saint, prophet, shrine ritual, halo, occult sign, fantasy glow, or religious monument; no European costume, colonial staging, caricature, duplicated faces, extra fingers, deformed hands, weapon spectacle, blood, corpse, gore, melodrama, clutter, or cropped lower title area.
